**The conclusion.** We have a strong, functioning implementation of the best extensively disclosed Phase 2 method family. We beat the adjusted score of the detailed 504aldo public release. We are substantially behind today's best submitted systems, and neither their code nor an academic recipe explaining their advantage was found. Our architecture is credible, but it has not been validated as sufficient to win. The next useful work is a targeted change to the third-order information the solver creates and preserves, supported by a diagnostic before another large training run.

**What the competition asks us to do.** A supplied network has 16 layers of 1,024 neurons, random fixed He-initialized weights, no biases, and ReLU activations. ReLU returns zero for a negative input and leaves a positive input unchanged. Imagine feeding this same network infinitely many independent standard-normal input vectors. The task is to predict the average output of every neuron. We receive the complete weight matrices, so we can reason about this distribution instead of estimating it only by running examples. A submitted estimator returns a 16 by 1,024 array. The target network is never trained; our estimator is the object we develop and fit. [Overview](https://www.aicrowd.com/challenges/arc-white-box-estimation-challenge-2026), [official rules v14](https://www.aicrowd.com/challenges/arc-white-box-estimation-challenge-2026/challenge_rules).

The first layer has a closed-form answer: the mean activation of a neuron is the length of its incoming weight vector divided by sqrt(2 pi). Later layers are harder because their inputs are correlated and non-Gaussian. ReLU repeatedly changes the distribution's shape. The central challenge is preserving enough of that shape to predict tiny changes in the eventual means without paying for an enormous simulation.

**How the score works.** For each supplied network, the grader computes the mean squared prediction error over the 1,024 neurons in the final layer. It multiplies that error by `max(0.1, F / 2^41)`, where F is counted arithmetic. It then averages those per-network products. Lower is better. If arithmetic usage varies across networks, multiplying overall mean error by overall mean cost is not the exact scoring rule. Intermediate-layer predictions remain required but do not directly determine rank. [Official scoring rules](https://www.aicrowd.com/challenges/arc-white-box-estimation-challenge-2026/challenge_rules).

At unchanged error, reducing usage from 20% to 10% of budget halves the score. Reducing it from 10% to 5% has no further score benefit. Sampling error commonly decreases roughly as 1/N while sampling work increases as N, so merely doubling the samples often fails to improve the product above the floor. This does not mean computation is irrelevant to analytical estimators such as ours.

The arithmetic cap is 2^41, approximately 2.20 trillion FLOPs per network. Additional limits are 120 seconds total wall time, 0.4 seconds residual Python/control overhead, and 8 GiB process memory. In Phase 2 residual overhead has zero price in the score, but exceeding its cap still fails a network. Grading is CPU-only. Our RTX 5090 helps offline fitting and research; it is not a resource the submitted estimator receives. The supplied process gets one physical core, with seven more physical cores allocated to the metered backend/harness. [Rules](https://www.aicrowd.com/challenges/arc-white-box-estimation-challenge-2026/challenge_rules), [Phase 2 launch](https://discourse.aicrowd.com/t/phase-2-of-the-arc-white-box-estimation-challenge-is-live/18197).

All final-layer raw MSEs quoted in this review should be read with the organizer-reference noise floor of approximately **7.7e-11**, measured locally on 20 FIT networks; the exact floor on undisclosed scored networks is not available. This is about 0.35% of our current raw error and cannot explain the leader gap. Layer 1 has a different floor, approximately 6.8e-10. The old layer-1 estimate must not be applied to the final layer. **What our system is.** The submitted program is a deterministic statistical solver with small learned corrections. It carries a compact description of the distribution through each layer:

| State | Intuitive meaning | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Mean vector | Each neuron's average activation | The quantity we eventually submit |
| Covariance matrix | Which pairs fluctuate together | Correlation changes subsequent weighted sums |
| Factored third-order history | Asymmetric joint fluctuations involving up to three neurons | Important departures from a Gaussian distribution affect later means |
| Approximate fourth-order information | Selected additional shape/dependence corrections | Improves nonlinear transformation formulas |
| Small fitted corrector | A learned adjustment to formula errors | Compensates recurring approximation bias |

For each layer, it transports the stored information through the given weights, approximates the ReLU transformation, corrects the means, creates new third-order contributions, and compresses old history. It retains five recent source generations in full-width factored form and moves older sources into a shared rank-256 basis. A separate birth residual is compressed to rank four. A per-layer 33→32→32→1 neural corrector has about 34,832 parameters across the 16 layers. The final layer avoids computing state that no later layer consumes. [Source-grounded architecture audit](2026-09-27_architecture_audit_codex.md).

The term “exact tower” in some comments describes identities inside this representation; the full method is approximate. Nonlinear truncation, compressed history, the low-rank residual, and modeled fourth-order terms all lose information. The current system already trains through a differentiable version of the actual estimator computation. PyTorch is used offline; the submission contains permitted flopscope/stdlib operations and fitted constants. Strassen-Winograd multiplication reduces the arithmetic bill. These are meaningful engineering achievements, not merely a starter model with additional training.

**The public and academic frontier.** The strongest detailed Phase 2 implementation disclosure located is [504aldo's factored third-cumulant estimator and 25 measured dead ends](https://discourse.aicrowd.com/t/everything-we-tried-a-factorized-k-3-cumulant-propagation-estimator-at-0-25-x-b-where-its-flops-go-and-25-measured-dead-ends-team-504aldo-rank-10/18218), with [public code](https://github.com/504aldo/whest-p2-cumulant-k3). We are ahead of its disclosed adjusted score. The quoted raw errors do not establish an accuracy advance over it; our primary advantage in that comparison is cost. Its old “rank 10” title is historical, not today's position. Current leaders are clearly ahead of that disclosed result, but their scores do not identify their algorithms.

The directly relevant academic foundation is [Wu et al., Estimating the expected output of wide random MLPs more efficiently than sampling](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.05179). It develops the cumulant-propagation family our system uses. Its theoretical assumptions, error normalization, and FLOP accounting differ from the current competition, so its plots are not hidden leaderboard scores. Its implementation notes [explicitly acknowledge omitted same-order terms in factorized AUGMENT](https://github.com/alignment-research-center/mlp_cumulant_propagation/blob/main/TODO.md). That gives a concrete mathematical reason to inspect selected missing interactions; it does not justify restoring every expensive term.

The broader review covered finite-width Edgeworth/para-Gaussian approximations, learned moment closures, solver-in-the-loop training, permutation-equivariant neural functionals, implicit symmetric CP decompositions, and tensor sketches. The key limitation is that papers averaging over random network weights do not establish an exact closure for our particular fixed network with random inputs. Learned-closure and sketching papers provide design tools, not a demonstrated winning estimator. ReLU respects neuron permutations, but not arbitrary rotations, so some attractive symmetry analogies in earlier notes are too broad. Full references and specific transfer limitations are in the [literature audit](2026-09-27_literature_audit_codex.md).

No reviewed source revealed the current leaders' complete method or an academic estimator with a directly comparable verified Phase 2 score beating them. Our uncertainty is about the mechanism behind their advantage, not whether that advantage exists.

**What a winning improvement has to accomplish.** At our current error, reaching the scoring floor would still give approximately **2.1906e-9**, worse than J2W's 1.761e-9. At our current cost, reaching that score would require raw error around **8.20e-9**, roughly a 63% reduction. Even a hypothetical perfect, zero-cost old-history tier only moves cost from 21.47% to about 15.45%, yielding approximately **3.38e-9** at unchanged error. These are score-law calculations, not measured candidates. A story that the leaders merely made our old tier free is insufficient.

The practical aim should be a joint accuracy/cost advance. A score near 1.7e-9 would compete with today's leader; it would not guarantee first place on October 17 or in the fresh rerun. Reliability must remain part of architecture selection because a fast public score is worthless if the program fails in the final environment.

**The recommended design direction for the next plan.** Keep the functioning v68 solver as the control. Prioritize the joint fluctuations of three distinct neurons: how they are created by ReLU, carried through subsequent weights, and compressed. The failed marginal/pair correction networks did not directly change this full three-neuron object. The audit derived a way to inspect its contribution using already available labels, without building a 1,024-cubed tensor: compute the next layer's complete third moment, subtract the contribution from one-neuron and repeated-pair moments, and compare the remaining contraction with our factors. Sampling uncertainty and centering conventions need explicit checks. This diagnostic separates all-distinct content from repeated-index content; it does not alone separate newly born error from inherited error.

A first inexpensive candidate is a small state-dependent gate on the existing third-order birth or transport factors. In plain language, let the strength of a modeled three-neuron interaction depend on the neuron's current distribution, rather than only on a layer-wide fitted number. This adds flexibility to an object S5 did not modify while preserving the existing factor count. Test birth-only and transport-only separately, start at exact baseline behavior, and preserve the repeated-index compensation algebra. Fit through the complete estimator, with an equal-training control. There is no measured gain yet; missing tensor directions might be impossible to recover by rescaling existing factors. [Detailed identities and source locations](2026-09-27_architecture_audit_codex.md).

For cost, investigate a compact nonradial remainder only after inspecting whether its relevant contractions are compressible. A singular-spectrum screen can reject implausibly small ranks before building an expensive online decomposer. Existing rank and harmonic failures are strong warnings. Selected omitted connected terms are another conditional branch, with their effect priced before integration. Generic tensor sketches are lower priority until an inexpensive complete update law through arbitrary weight matrices is derived. Matrix layout remains useful for runtime and memory, but a faster wall time helps score only when it enables a better permitted estimator; prior leaf16 experiments actually increased billed cost.

This gives the next design discussion a concrete basis: repair evaluation semantics; measure the missing third-order contribution; test one cheap factor correction; and pursue a cost representation only when its information content survives a small diagnostic. Continue incremental fitting as a separate near-term improvement, without confusing a sub-percent gain with evidence of a winning architecture. The contribution write-up should preserve these measured mechanisms and failures as research evidence alongside any eventual successful method.
