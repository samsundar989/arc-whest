# G. Closure-learning literature: calibrating the systematic residual of a cumulant-propagation closure

Scope: fast targeted sweep (15 searches, 20 fetches, 2026-09-26). Context: deterministic cumulant propagation (mean, C, κ3, approx. κ4) through a width-1024 / depth-16 random ReLU MLP with a truncated Edgeworth/Hermite closure; per-layer state errors grow *linearly* with depth (systematic closure bias), and are not predictable from per-neuron local features but plausibly are structured functions of the state, ΔC ≈ Σ_k c_k B_k(state). Goal: (1) supervised "closure learning" of that residual from exact 1e9-sample moment labels; (2) situate it in the right literature.

One-line diagnosis that the literature supports: a truncated-cumulant closure is a *cumulant-neglect closure*; its error is a deterministic function of the current state (the neglected cumulants are themselves functions of the tracked ones in the large-width limit), so it accumulates additively layer by layer rather than as a random walk — exactly the linear-in-depth signature observed. That makes the residual a legitimate object to model, not noise to average out.

## 1. Machine-learned / data-driven moment closures

**Huang, Hu, Ma et al., "Machine learning moment closure models for the radiative transfer equation" I–IV** — [I (JCP 2022)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0021999122000031), [II: global hyperbolicity (arXiv 2105.14410)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14410), [III: hyperbolicity + physical characteristic speeds (arXiv 2109.00700)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.00700), [IV: symmetrizable hyperbolicity in 2D (arXiv 2604.20143)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20143).
What is learned: not the unclosed moment itself but the *gradient* of the unclosed highest moment as a linear combination of gradients of the lower moments, with NN-learned coefficients that depend on the local lower moments. Stability: paper I found that a freely-learned closure was accurate offline but unstable in time; II–IV derive algebraic constraints on the learned coefficients (existence of a symmetrizer / SPD matrix) so the closed system is globally hyperbolic and inherits dissipativity and the correct diffusion limit; then long-time stability and out-of-regime generalization follow.
Transfers: the "learn coefficients on a fixed structural basis, constrain the coefficients so the closed system keeps a structural property" recipe is precisely ΔC ≈ Σ_k c_k(state) B_k(state) with constraints (C stays PSD, κ3 stays symmetric, correction → 0 in the Gaussian/large-width limit).

**Maddu, Weady, Shelley, "Learning fast, accurate, and stable closures of a kinetic theory of an active fluid" (arXiv 2308.06675)** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.06675).
What is learned: the unclosed fourth-moment contraction S:T as coefficients on a tensor basis {I, D, E} (isotropic tensor-function form); inputs are rotational invariants {tr D², tr E², tr DE}, not raw components — two orders of magnitude better than quadratic closure. Stability: purely supervised (snapshot) closures were unstable online; training through the solver over short windows of q = 2–4 time steps ("MPC-style" discrete time-stepping constraint) produced stable long rollouts. MLPs beat CNN/FNO — non-local inputs unnecessary.
Transfers: closest analogue to our setting. Use invariants of (C, κ3, κ4-proxy) as inputs, a state-derived tensor basis as output structure, and train through 2–4 layers of the propagator.

**Ling, Kurzawski, Templeton, "Reynolds averaged turbulence modelling using deep neural networks with embedded invariance" (JFM 2016)** — [OSTI](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/1333570); modern follow-ups: [TBNN without baseline models (JFM 2025)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-fluid-mechanics/article/abs/datadriven-turbulence-closure-using-a-tensor-basis-neural-network-without-reliance-on-baseline-models/059C616355F25DE075E4768F3B0AFDA0), [physics-informed TBNN (ML4PS 2023)](https://ml4physicalsciences.github.io/2023/files/NeurIPS_ML4PS_2023_91.pdf).
What is learned: Reynolds-stress anisotropy = Σ_k g_k(invariants) T_k, with T_k Pope's integrity basis; invariance is embedded by construction; beats unstructured NNs on accuracy and, critically, generalization.
Transfers: this is the canonical citation for "learned scalar coefficients on a fixed equivariant tensor basis" — the exact form of our hypothesised ΔC. For our problem the natural basis under orthogonal reparametrisation of the layer is {C, C², diag-corrections, κ3-contractions such as κ3:μ, κ3:κ3, ...}.

**Gupta & Lermusiaux, "Neural closure models for dynamical systems" (Proc. R. Soc. A 2021, arXiv 2012.13869)** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13869); [generalized/interpretable version (2023)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10313723/).
What is learned: an additive closure term on top of a known low-fidelity model, as a neural ODE/DDE; Mori–Zwanzig motivates non-Markovian (delay) closures, which beat Markovian ones for long-horizon accuracy; trained via adjoints through rolled-out trajectories.
Transfers: (a) the additive-residual-to-a-known-model framing; (b) the Mori–Zwanzig argument that a closure error that depends on untracked variables (κ4, κ5) is best approximated by a *memory* term — for us, feeding the previous layer's state/residual as input to the correction is principled, not a hack.

**Schotthöfer, Xiao, Frank, Hauck, "Data-driven, structure-preserving approximations to entropy-based moment closures" (2022)** — [OSTI 1999103](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/1999103); [regularized version (JCP 2025)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0021999125002505). Learn the convex entropy functional of the moment system with convex (input-convex NN / convex spline) approximants; convexity buys hyperbolicity, entropy dissipation and realizability automatically; training points sampled from the realizable set. Transfers: parameterize the correction so realizability of the corrected state is automatic (e.g. correct via a Cholesky factor, or learn a convex potential whose Hessian is the corrected C).

**Burles & Camporeale, "The machine learning approach to moment closure relations for plasma: a review" (arXiv 2511.22486)** — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22486). Survey; the recurring lessons: closures that are accurate offline accumulate error and destabilize online, generalization beyond the training distribution is the main failure mode, and off-diagonal (pressure-tensor) components are the hardest — the same component that is worst for us.

Also: [Hyperbolic ML moment closures for BGK (arXiv 2401.04783)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04783) — output layer engineered to enforce hyperbolicity and Galilean invariance; [Naghnaeian & Del Vecchio, "Robust moment closure method for the chemical master equation" (arXiv 1707.00178)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.00178) — calibrate an *affine* closure by LP with a worst-case ℓ∞ error bound; a precedent for "calibrated closure with a certificate".

## 2. Edgeworth / Gram–Charlier and calibrated closures

**Wu & Lin, "On the cumulant-neglect closure method in stochastic dynamics" (IJNLM 1996)** — [link](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0020746296000297); tutorial: [Matis & Kiffe, "Achieving moment closure through cumulant neglect"](https://www.mathematica-journal.com/2010/09/20/achieving-moment-closure-through-cumulant-neglect/). Key finding: raising the neglect order does not guarantee convergence; higher-order cumulant-neglect can be *less* accurate or unstable. Transfers: the honest citation for why we calibrate the order-4 truncation instead of going to order 6.

**Copula-based Gaussian-mixture closure (Prob. Eng. Mech. 2019)** — [link](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0266892019302127). Replaces Gram–Charlier (which yields non-positive densities and poor tails) by a positive family with the same low-order moments. Transfers: the alternative to Edgeworth for the ReLU closure integrals — a two-component Gaussian mixture matched to (μ, σ², κ3, κ4) gives closed-form ReLU moments and no negative-density artefacts; a candidate "calibrated closure family".

**Levermore-type maximum-entropy closures and their ML approximations** — [affordable max-entropy closures (JCP 2013)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0021999113004130), [optimization of entropy-based closures (OSTI)](https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/1261246-optimization-large-scale-computation-entropy-based-moment-closure), plus Schotthöfer et al. above. Transfers: realizability language (the set of (κ2, κ3, κ4) reachable by a distribution) — our learned correction must map the realizable set to itself.

**Nica & Ortmann, "Improving the Gaussian approximation in neural networks: para-Gaussians and Edgeworth expansions"** — [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=92q7WV4od7). Directly on point: a 1/n Edgeworth-type correction to the Gaussian pre-activation law is *closed under a layer* (Theorem 2: para-Gaussian in → para-Gaussian out with an explicit recursion for the kernel K and correction C); error O(1/n²) vs O(1/n); corrections stay controlled when L/n is not small. Transfers: (a) the correction to the Gaussian closure is a structured, layer-recursive object, which is the theoretical justification for calibrating it as a state function; (b) the recursion is additive per layer to leading order — the mechanism behind linear-in-depth growth.

**Celli, "Optimal non-asymptotic Edgeworth expansions for multivariate neural network outputs" (arXiv 2605.24072)** — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.24072), [code comparing MC densities to Edgeworth expansions of different degree](https://github.com/LuciaCelli/Simulation_Edgeworth_Expansion_for_FCNNs). Recent multivariate, non-asymptotic Edgeworth bounds for FCNN outputs around the NNGP limit. Transfers: citation for the accuracy/degree trade-off of Edgeworth closures at finite width; the simulation code is a ready-made check of degree-k truncation error against MC.

Related finite-width recursions (exact targets our κ4 approximation should reproduce): [Yaida, "Non-Gaussian processes and neural networks at finite widths" (MSML 2020)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v107/yaida20a/yaida20a.pdf); [Antognini, "Finite size corrections for NNGPs" (arXiv 1908.10030)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.10030); [Random ReLU networks as non-Gaussian processes (arXiv 2405.10229)](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.10229v1).

## 3. Random / deep networks: covariance through ReLU, ADF, learned covariance corrections

**Wu, Nowozin, Meeds, Lakshminarayanan, Turner et al., "Deterministic variational inference for robust Bayesian neural networks" (ICLR 2019, arXiv 1810.03958)** — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.03958), [full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.03958). Propagates mean and *full covariance* through ReLU/Heaviside layers with closed forms (soft-ReLU asymptote term SR(μ1)SR(μ2)+ρΦ(μ1)Φ(μ2) plus an arcsin-ρ − ρ quadratic correction Q); assumes Gaussian pre-activations by CLT; the quadratic truncation of Q and the Gaussian assumption are the stated approximations, validated empirically against MC (Sec. 3.1.1). Transfers: the ReLU covariance map is analytic under Gaussianity, so *all* residual error is attributable to (i) non-Gaussian pre-activations and (ii) the truncation of the covariance kernel — both functions of the state; good citation for "the Gaussian-closure baseline and its known error sources".

**Gast & Roth, "Lightweight probabilistic deep networks" (CVPR 2018)** — ADF with mean/variance propagation through ReLU (Frey–Hinton moments); diagonal only. Surveys of the sampling-free-propagation family: [Uncertainty propagation in feed-forward NN models (arXiv 2503.21059)](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21059), [Stable distribution propagation (ICLR 2024, arXiv 2402.08324)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.08324), [MP-GELU moment propagation (arXiv 2211.13402)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.13402). Note: no paper found that *learns* a residual on top of moment propagation through ReLU — that gap is our contribution.

**KalmanNet and Recursive KalmanNet** — [KalmanNet (Revach et al., IEEE TSP 2022)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/353374958_KalmanNet_Neural_Network_Aided_Kalman_Filtering_for_Partially_Known_Dynamics), [Recursive KalmanNet (arXiv 2506.11639)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11639), [Unscented KalmanNet (arXiv 2608.04201)](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.04201). Keep the filter's algebraic structure, learn only the gain (from innovation/state-difference features), and — in the recursive version — propagate covariance with Joseph's form so the learned part cannot make it inconsistent; trained with Gaussian NLL on whole sequences. Transfers: the "learn the small thing, keep the structure that guarantees consistency" pattern, and sequence-level (rolled-out) training.

## 4. Training a correction inside an iterated model (rollout / DAgger family)

**Um, Brand, Fei, Holl, Thuerey, "Solver-in-the-loop" (NeurIPS 2020, arXiv 2007.00016)** — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00016), [code](https://github.com/tum-pbs/Solver-in-the-loop). Learned correction of a coarse solver toward a reference; one-step supervised training (PRE) vs training with the differentiable solver unrolled (SOL). SOL strongly wins because the correction sees the states the *corrected* solver actually produces; stable rollouts of hundreds of steps; more unrolled steps generally better (variants unroll from a few to on the order of a hundred steps).

**Brandstetter, Worrall, Welling, "Message passing neural PDE solvers" (ICLR 2022, arXiv 2202.03376)** — [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.03376). The *pushforward trick*: roll out one step with no gradient (so the input is drawn from the model's own pushforward distribution A♯p), then compute the one-step loss on the next step with gradient; explicitly do **not** backprop through the first step ("faster and more stable"). Framed as zero-stability: minimize the amplification κ of self-generated perturbations. *Temporal bundling*: predict K steps per call to reduce the number of distribution shifts. See also [PDE-Refiner (NeurIPS 2023)](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/d529b943af3dba734f8a7d49efcb6d09-Paper-Conference.pdf) and the [ICLR 2023 blog "Autoregressive renaissance"](https://iclr-blogposts.github.io/2023/blog/2023/autoregressive-neural-pde-solver/) for a survey of these tricks.

**Sanchez-Gonzalez et al., "Learning to simulate complex physics with graph networks" (ICML 2020)** — [pdf](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/sanchez-gonzalez20a/sanchez-gonzalez20a.pdf). One-step training plus random-walk noise injection on the inputs ("the training distribution is closer to the distribution generated during rollouts") is enough for thousand-step stability; no BPTT. Underlying principle: DAgger (Ross, Gordon, Bagnell 2011) — aggregate training states from the learner's own rollouts.

Practical recipe for our loop (distilled): (1) generate the supervision on states *produced by the current corrected propagator*, not on exact states (pushforward/DAgger) — otherwise the correction is fit to inputs it never sees; (2) short windows of 2–4 layers with gradient only through the last (Brandstetter) or through the whole window (Um, Maddu); (3) alternatively one-step training with state-noise whose covariance matches the observed residual (Sanchez-Gonzalez); (4) the correction is additive, small, and structurally constrained; (5) the selection metric is end-of-depth error, never one-step error. Our exact 1e9-sample labels make the supervision unusually clean — the main risk is distribution mismatch, not label noise.

## 5. Symmetric CP truncation of third cumulants (quick look only)

**Sherman & Kolda, "Estimating higher-order moments using symmetric tensor decomposition" (SIMAX 2020, arXiv 1911.03813)** — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.03813), [OSTI](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1725841). Implicit symmetric CP fit of d-th moment tensors from samples in O(pnr) per iteration without forming the n^d tensor. Related: [approximate diagonalization of symmetric 3-tensors by orthogonal transforms (arXiv 1804.01358)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.01358), [subspace power method for symmetric tensor decomposition (Kileel & Pereira, IPAM talk)](https://www.ipam.ucla.edu/abstract/?tid=17234). Elementary fact that makes this usable: a symmetric CP form Σ_k λ_k v_k^{⊗3} transported by a linear layer W becomes Σ_k λ_k (Wv_k)^{⊗3} exactly (rank preserved, cost O(r·n²)); only the ReLU closure raises the rank, so re-truncation to rank r after each nonlinearity is where all the κ3 error enters. No paper was found in the time budget that tracks third cumulants of a linear–nonlinear chain in symmetric CP format with truncation; treat this as an open, small, citable contribution if we do it.

## How to frame "calibrated closure corrections" as a mechanistic contribution

The write-up should present the learned residual as a *calibrated closure*, in the tradition of Huang–Hu–Ma, Maddu–Weady–Shelley and Ling et al., rather than as a black-box patch, and three facts earn that framing. First, the object being corrected is identified: the estimator is an order-4 cumulant-neglect closure of an exact moment hierarchy, and the neglected terms are — by the para-Gaussian/Edgeworth recursion (Nica–Ortmann; Yaida) — deterministic functions of the tracked state at leading order in 1/n, so the residual is structured and layer-additive, which is exactly what the linear-in-depth diagnostics show. Second, the correction is written in an equivariant tensor-basis form ΔC = Σ_k c_k(I) B_k(state), with B_k built from the state (C, C², κ3-contractions, the DVI arcsin kernel terms) and c_k depending only on invariants; this is the TBNN/Pope construction, it gives interpretable coefficients (each c_k reports how much of a named neglected term was missing), and it should be shown that c_k → 0 as width → ∞ and that the correction preserves realizability (PSD C, symmetric κ3). Third, the calibration protocol follows the solver-in-the-loop / pushforward practice: coefficients are fit on states generated by the corrected propagator, with the end-of-depth exact-moment error as the selection criterion, and reported with held-out width/depth generalization — which is the evidence that distinguishes a closure model from curve fitting. Stated this way, the contribution is "we identify the leading closure bias of cumulant propagation through ReLU layers and calibrate it with a few structured, invariant-dependent coefficients," which reads as mechanism, is falsifiable (the coefficients have predicted scalings), and slots into an established literature.
