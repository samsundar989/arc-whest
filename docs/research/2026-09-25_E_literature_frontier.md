## 0. Executive summary (what matters for us in the next three weeks)

1. **Phase 2 is exactly the companion paper's sweet spot.** Paper's headline experiments: width 256, 4 hidden layers ⇒ L/n = 1/64. Phase 2: 1024 × 16 ⇒ L/n = 1/64. ## 1. The companion paper — arXiv:2605.05179

**Wu, Lecomte, Winer, Robinson, Hilton, Christiano, "Estimating the expected output of wide random MLPs more efficiently than sampling."** Abstract: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05179 · HTML: https://arxiv.org/html/2605.05179 · Code: https://github.com/alignment-research-center/mlp_cumulant_propagation · ARC blog: https://www.alignment.org/blog/mechanistic-estimation-for-wide-random-mlps/ · LW/AF cross-post: https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/fsG4m6sRMpomd7Rk6/mechanistic-estimation-for-wide-random-mlps

**Abstract (verbatim):** "By far the most common way to estimate an expected loss in machine learning is to draw samples, compute the loss on each one, and take the empirical average. However, sampling is not necessarily optimal. Given an MLP at initialization, we show how to estimate its expected output over Gaussian inputs without running samples through the network at all. Instead, we produce approximate representations of the distributions of activations at each layer, leveraging tools such as cumulants and Hermite expansions. We show both theoretically and empirically that for sufficiently wide networks, our estimator achieves a target mean squared error using substantially fewer FLOPs than Monte Carlo sampling. We find moreover that our methods perform particularly well at estimating the probabilities of rare events, and additionally demonstrate how they can be used for model training. Together, these findings suggest a path to producing models with a greatly reduced probability of catastrophic tail risks."

### 1.1 Methods

- **kprop (cumulant propagation).** Track cumulants of the activation distribution up to order K through each layer. Linear layers act by multilinearity (einsum of the k-th cumulant with k copies of W). ReLU layers are handled by the **diagram summation formula** (Thm A.2): cumulants of φ(Z_1..Z_n) from cumulants of Z and Hermite coefficients of φ, summed over connected set-partition diagrams ("cluster-expansion diagrams from statistical physics", hyperedges for higher orders; tensor-network diagrams appear in Appendix A). K=1 = mean propagation (+ trace of covariance); K=2 = full mean+covariance; K=3/4 add non-Gaussian structure.
- **Power cumulants (the essential trick).** Instead of Hermite-expanding φ, expand *powers* of φ and track κ[X_i^{α1},…,X_m^{αm}] with distinct indices; recover ordinary cumulants by the partition formula and its inverse. Ablating this makes the error O(1) instead of O(1/n^K) (Fig. 3b).
- **SIMPLE vs AUGMENT.** Basic version tracks cumulants ≤ K plus, for odd K, one scalar: the *full trace* of the (K+1)-th cumulant tensor. Augmented version tracks "the entire [(K+1)-th] tensor except for its traceless component" — better MSE, higher cost.
- **Factored.** For K ≥ 3 the K-th cumulant tensor admits a factorized representation of size n^(K-1) instead of n^K; "improve the runtime … by a factor of n … without changing its final output." Code: `factor_k3.py`, `factor_k4.py`.
- **Harmonic / diagonal-slice decompositions** (code, not paper body): `HTensor` (symmetric tensor as decomposition into harmonics) and `DSTensor` (diagonal slices); `kprop_harmonic.py` "alternates between the harmonic and diagonal slice decompositions"; `kprop_ds.py` is a deprecated slice-only version. `flop_utils.py` counts FLOPs "assuming a fictitious symmetric tensor kernel" (adjustment α_{n,d} = C(n+d−1,d)/n^d, Appendix J).
- Pseudocode for K=1,2 in Appendix B; `symb/` does symbolic cumulant propagation; `wick.py` holds Hermite ("Wick") coefficients of activations.

### 1.2 Reported numbers

- **Theorem 5.1 (basic):** polynomial activations, fixed L, He weights; for noticeable ε(n), MSE = O(ε²) in time O(n²/ε²) — matches MC (Θ(n²/ε²)).
- **Theorem 5.2 (factorized):** if additionally ε(n)² = O(1/n²), time O(n/ε²) — an n-fold speed-up.
- **Empirical (ReLU, width 256):** factorized and factorized-augmented beat MC by ~100–1000×; 2 hidden layers ~1000×; 4 hidden layers 10–100× ("outperform Monte Carlo sampling at FLOP budgets spanning 7 orders of magnitude, in some cases achieving the same mean squared error using fewer than 1/100 as many FLOPs" — ARC blog); at 8 hidden layers K=4 underperforms MC.
- **Width scaling (Fig. 3a):** MSE ∝ n^(−K). **Depth scaling (Fig. 8):** error grows roughly like L^K; conjecture MSE ≤ c_K (L/n)^K. At width 256: K=1 competitive to 12 layers, K=2 to ~8, K=3–4 mainly 2–4 layers.
- **Wall clock (Fig. 11):** slower than MC in practice; blamed on Python overhead.
- Rare events: K=3 keeps <30% relative error at probabilities 100× below 1/N of a matched MC run. Distillation (K=2) competitive with SGD.

### 1.3 Open problems / future work (verbatim, §7.3)

"There is enormous scope for further research on mechanistic estimation. Even for random MLPs, we do not know whether it is possible to match the performance of random sampling at large depth (i.e., asymptotically as the number of hidden layers L tends to infinity), or for narrow networks (i.e., asymptotically as the error tolerance ε → 0 while the width n is held fixed). We could also ask the same questions for MLPs with weights drawn from non-independent or non-Gaussian distributions." … "Beyond random MLPs, learned MLPs pose a key challenge … Another key challenge is to extend beyond closed-form input distributions to empirical input distributions for which we only have black-box access." §7.2: adapting to partially trained nets "may need … a 'structure versus randomness' dichotomy."

Related work acknowledged: Fischer et al. 2022 (covariance-only), Wright et al. 2024 (ReLU covariance formulas). **No learned/fitted corrections appear in the paper.**

**Relevance:** our chain (mean + cov + factored K3 + regenerated κ4 with per-layer λ) is the paper's factorized-augmented-K3 with the augmented (K+1) channel replaced by a closure. The paper says augmented tracks the (K+1)-tensor *minus its traceless part*; our λ·C_pre regeneration is a one-parameter model of exactly that non-traceless part — consistent with 504aldo's finding that a single scalar per layer gets R² ≥ 0.95 from layer 3 onward.

## 5. Academic literature — what each offers and whether it is beyond the leaders

**Triage rule (from 504aldo, worth adopting):** (1) annealed over weights ⇒ κ3 = 0 by construction, useless for a quenched (fixed-weight) problem; (2) if quenched, does it write cross-neuron cumulants of order ≥3? (3) does a cost saving act on a factored representation or only on a dense n^d tensor that is never materialised? (4) does it report moment error vs depth at ≤1e-6?

| Work | Offers | Beyond leaders? |
|---|---|---|
| **Cho & Saul 2009**, arc-cosine kernels (http://papers.neurips.cc/paper/3628-kernel-methods-for-deep-learning.pdf); Daniely et al. 2016 dual activations | Closed-form E[ReLU(u·x)ReLU(v·x)] = ‖u‖‖v‖/(2π)[sin θ + (π−θ)cos θ]; Hermite coefficients of ReLU. | No — this is the K=2 closure everyone uses (starter kit example 03; marius_binner's layer-2 "snap"). |
| **Wright & Ma 2024**, "An Analytic Solution to Covariance Propagation in Neural Networks" (AISTATS; https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16163) | Exact bivariate covariance through Heaviside/ReLU/GELU; cited by the ARC paper. | No (K=2). venture_agent_ai's exact bivariate route gave only 7.4% over the kit. |
| **"Exact Gaussian Moment Matching for Residual Networks: a Second-Order Method"** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22307) | ReLU as GeLU limit; "up to a millionfold" KL improvements on *random* networks vs prior moment matching; second-order error bound. | No new object beyond K=2, but its error-bound framing ("moment matching removes the leading low-variance errors and propagates higher-order local accuracy") is a clean way to write up our closure-error analysis. |
| **Uncertainty propagation through trained MLPs: exact results** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16830) | Exact mean/variance for one hidden ReLU layer. | No. |
| **Wu et al. 2019 DVI** (https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.03958); **Hernández-Lobato & Adams 2015 PBP** (https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.05336) | Deterministic moment propagation (mean+cov / diagonal ADF) for BNNs; ReLU moment formulas. | No; superseded by K2 + K3. Useful only as citations for the "moment propagation" lineage. |
| **Fischer, Lindner, Dahmen, Helias 2022**, PRR 4, 043143 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.04925) | Networks as maps between correlation functions; explicit "how the non-linearity transfers information between orders"; up to 2nd order in hidden layers; quenched trained weights. | No for order ≥3 (they truncate at 2), but the *diagrammatic bookkeeping* of order-mixing is the same machinery as ARC's diagram sum. |
| **Yaida 2020** (https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.00019); **Roberts–Yaida–Hanin PDLT** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10165); **Hanin–Nica 2019** (https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.05994); **Zavatone-Veth & Pehlevan 2021** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.11734) | Finite-width 1/n expansions, four-point vertex recursions, depth-to-width ratio L/n as the expansion parameter, exact Meijer-G priors. | **Annealed** (averaged over weights) ⇒ κ3 = 0; not directly usable. Conceptually they justify why Phase 2 (L/n = 1/64) is perturbative and Phase 1 (1/8) was not, and why per-layer fitted constants may scale with L/n. |
| **"Optimal Non-Asymptotic Edgeworth Expansions for Multivariate NN Outputs"** (https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.24072, May 2026) | Edgeworth expansions of order 4m−1 with n^−m TV error, matching lower bounds; code on GitHub. | Annealed; theoretical. Confirms Edgeworth-in-1/n is the right asymptotic language but does not give a quenched algorithm. |
| **Borel resummation** of divergent Hermite/Edgeworth closures | ARC's townhall hint. | No public implementation for this problem; speculative but ARC-endorsed direction. |
| **Large-deviation / DMFT / field theory of random RNNs** (van Meegen–Kühn–Helias 2021; Segadlo et al. 2022; Bordelon–Pehlevan) | Saddle-point statistics over weight ensembles. | Annealed; 504aldo's literature triage (~25 papers) found "zero applicable methods." |
| **Strassen–Winograd** (7 mults, 15 adds) and **alternative-basis SW** (Karstadt & Schwartz, SPAA'17/JACM'19, https://www.cs.huji.ac.il/~odedsc/papers/SPAA17-MatMul-a-Little-Faster.pdf; "fast and stable" IPDPS'24) | 12 additions/level instead of 15 (proven optimal), basis change O(n² log n); leading coefficient 6→5. | **Marginal but real.** Under flopscope (adds = numel), five-level SW additions are ≈7% of the metered product cost at n = 1024 (skibidi measured 13.7% of its bill at n = 256); alternative basis removes 20% of that ⇒ ≈1–2% net after basis transforms. Leaders are unlikely to be exploiting this. |
| **4×4 in 48 multiplications** (AlphaEvolve 2025 complex; Dumas–Pernet–Sedoglavic rational version https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13242; "more accurate" version March 2026 https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18699); **3×3 rank-23 with 59 additions** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05272) | Fewer leaf products per two levels (48 vs 49 = −2%). | **Not useful at n = 1024.** The 48-algorithm's operation count is ≈9.875 n^2.7925 (paper's own bound) versus SW's ≈6 n^2.807; at n = 1024 that is ≈2.5e9 vs ≈1.7e9 (dense ≈2.1e9), i.e. its addition overhead dominates at these sizes. 3×3;23 has exponent 2.854 > 2.807. 504aldo also found Strassen on lone products "≤4% on paper, 0% shippable" because of residual-time cost. |
| **Symmetric-contraction FLOP savings** (Solomonik–Demmel) | Halve symmetric contractions. | Not applicable: no dense symmetric order-3 object is ever formed (504aldo #13); flopscope only discounts repeated-operand einsums. |
| **Randomized range-finders / tensor sketching** (Halko–Martinsson–Tropp; Pham–Pagh tensor sketch; randomized CP) | Cheap low-rank bases for transported legs; sketched cumulants for training data. | Already used (504aldo's rank-384/224 shared bases; keenanpepper's Ω-sketches). The **untested** variant is choosing the basis by *downstream* weights (tim8es #17), i.e. a two-sided / adjoint-weighted range finder. |
| **Hermite/polynomial-chaos degree spectrum; spherical designs (Kerdock/MUB; Delsarte–Goethals–Seidel)** | Explains the sampling floor (64% of integrand variance at degree ≥6). | Only relevant to sampling routes, which are dead at 1024×16 (pure sampling ≈1.2e-6 adj). |
| **Christiano–Neyman–Xu 2022, "Formalizing the presumption of independence"** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.06738) | Heuristic-argument framing; mean propagation as the base case. | Background for the write-up's motivation; no algorithm. |

## 7. Candidate ideas beyond the current frontier — ranked by evidence

**Rank 1 — Get to the 0.1 floor first (evidence: scoring rule + nkosi_ndwandwe + board).** If we are at raw ≈2e-8 and ≈0.25 × B, every FLOP saved until 0.1 × B is worth up to 2.5× on the score; after that, zero. 504aldo's public V29 shows the legal path 0.51→0.25 (Strassen 5 levels, batched families, `out=` buffers, final-layer trim, float32 cast on entry). The remaining 0.25→0.10 is exactly the "old sources" tier (107 of 260 units); see ranks 3–5. Also confirm we never exceed B on any MLP (hard cliff), keep residual < 0.4 s, setup < 5 s, and handle the grader's non-suite smoke shapes (depth 32 observed).

**Rank 2 — Trajectory-fitted, state-keyed learned corrections consuming cumulant features (organizer-confirmed).** Hilton: warm-up winners were factorized K3 "combined with learned networks that consume the cumulant estimates as features." Phase 2 leak: ridge + gradient boosting in a top stack. The public evidence on *how* to do it: corrections must be fitted on the chain's own rolled-forward trajectories (jamesrahenry, 8.4×), keyed by state (participation ratio, ReLU operating point |μ|/σ) rather than layer index; scalar terminal patches and per-MLP adaptivity are "transfer traps" (census; baltsat); nonlinear heads lose to bounded polynomials in α (radiant-allomancer); features that are cheap per-neuron statistics correlate <0.09 with the final residual at depth 16 (504aldo #10) — so the corrector must act *in-loop on the propagated state* (as ours does), not at the output. Training data is sanctioned (townhall) and abundant (keenanpepper bakes with dense K21/K22 at 1024×16). Sources: https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/Kben8CzS4awCwNw5c , /t/18217 , /t/18097 , /t/18157 , /t/18085 , /t/18218.

**Rank 3 — Covariance-response closure for κ4 with a few live modes (Puffi SSC; strong measured evidence at 256×32, plausibly what carries Puffi to #6 now).** κ̂4 = Σ Λ_ab (B_a ⊗ B_b + perms) with ≤4 response modes refreshed after each ReLU and a 4×4 Λ per layer fitted by ridge; −77% vs factorized K3 at 0.5 B, depth exponent 2.21 vs 2.69. Our κ4 regeneration `G_off = λ·C_pre_off` is the r = 1 special case (one mode = the covariance, one scalar). Upgrading to 2–4 modes with a small fitted Λ (and fitting Λ to the post-ReLU *mean correction* rather than marginal κ4, +13.6% in Puffi's audit) is a direct, cheap extension. Source: Puffi algorithmic PDF via https://discourse.aicrowd.com/t/18175.

**Rank 4 — Collapse the old-source tier (the leaders' unexplained 0.15 × B).** 504aldo's cost anatomy says leaders pay ≈0 for transported K3 history. Candidate mechanisms, none publicly confirmed: (a) one merged K3 object per layer in a *fixed* shared basis chosen by **downstream weights / adjoint sensitivity of the final mean** rather than forward energy (tim8es issue #17: "the accuracy-relevant K3 content lives in low-energy remainders"); (b) ARC's own harmonic (`HTensor`) representation, which stores symmetric tensors as harmonic components rather than per-source legs and may sidestep the Khatri–Rao "Hadamard wall"; (c) a memoryless K3 closure *plus* a fitted transported correction — 504aldo measured the pure memoryless floor at 9.1e-7 and Puffi's 144-constant correction at 1.07×, so this route needs a learned component (rank 2) to work. Sources: https://discourse.aicrowd.com/t/18218 , https://github.com/tim8es/arc-whitebox/issues/17 , ARC repo README.

**Rank 5 — Hot/cold (dead/on/kink) neuron routing inside the analytic chain.** In Phase 1 this cut the multiplier 6% for Cipo and 45% of compute for marius_binner at −1.9% MSE; ARC's townhall independently suggested "spending more compute on higher-variance directions." For a cumulant chain the analogue is exact: neurons with P(z>0) ≈ 1 pass ReLU as the identity (zero closure error, purely linear transport), P(z>0) ≈ 0 contribute nothing, so D21/K3 work can be confined to the "kink" subset, and always-on/dead columns can be folded into the transport. Nobody has published this for the analytic route in Phase 2. Sources: skibidi PDF, https://discourse.aicrowd.com/t/18106 , townhall summary.

**Rank 6 — Alternative-basis Strassen–Winograd (Karstadt–Schwartz).** 12 vs 15 block additions per level; basis transforms O(n² log n). Under flopscope's honest accounting this is ≈1–2% of the bill at n = 1024 — small, but "free" and rule-safe; only worthwhile if we are within 2% of the floor. Do **not** pursue 4×4;48 or 3×3;23 — their addition overhead makes them net-negative at these sizes. Sources: https://www.cs.huji.ac.il/~odedsc/papers/SPAA17-MatMul-a-Little-Faster.pdf , https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18699.

**Rank 7 — Final-layer specialisation and D21 feedback (already in the public frontier).** V19-style last-layer trim (only variance, D3, κ4-diag needed; −8% cost) and rank-16 D21 feedback into birth factors (+10% raw). If we lack either, they are the cheapest wins (https://discourse.aicrowd.com/t/18218).

**Rank 8 — Borel/Padé resummation of the ReLU Hermite closure.** ARC says Edgeworth "improvements plateau then diverge" and "Borel resummation shows promise but remains unsolved." No public code; high write-up value for the $20k algorithmic prize if it measurably helps, but risky for the score in three weeks. Source: https://discourse.aicrowd.com/t/18078.

**Rank 9 — Two-designated-submission hedging.** The rule allows two nominations, default = top-2 public. Nominate one max-accuracy and one conservative (guaranteed < 0.4 s residual, < B on every shape). Ask the organizers (arc-whestbench@aicrowd.com) whether the better of the two counts; the forum question is unanswered (https://discourse.aicrowd.com/t/18143).

**Dead ends to avoid (measured by others):** MC control-variate or antithetic sidecars (504aldo #12, thylinao 0.93×); per-source κ4 legs (≥0.95 × B); Tucker/CP caps on merged tensors (14–31× worse); per-MLP adaptive levers (held-out reversals); float64 hot paths (+0.004%); exact bivariate covariance (7.4%); accounting levers (flopscope billing is tight; manual DQ for unmetered compute).
