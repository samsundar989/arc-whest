## 1. Your questions

### 1.1 Are we using the optimal structures and cumulants?

**The coordinates are right.**
- With the true pre-activation variance, κ₃ marginal and joint κ₄ given at every layer, raw drops 20× (EscAI's oracle) [M].
- Our refit ceilings, with exact slices substituted at every layer and the model refit: κ₃ 53.4 %, covariance 40.5 %, κ₄ 27.9 %, everything but μ 91.4 % (RESULTS, Step 2) [M].
- So the Edgeworth readout is not the bottleneck; what we feed it is.

**The error lives in off-diagonal structure, not in anything local.**
- Single per-neuron features explain ≤ 0.7 % of each layer's residual. A 33-feature MLP's teacher-forced ceiling is 6.2–7.3 %, and our corrector already took −5.2 % [M].
- A learned closure gave ≤ 0.75 % and was score-negative. It was fitted offline on exact inputs (per-pair net on K11/K21, per-neuron net on K1–K4) and then fine-tuned in the chain (Step 3.3) [M].
- The error comes from κ₃'s all-distinct content, from the covariance and from Q31. Gains therefore have to come from the physics of the slices or from cost, not from more learning.

### 1.2 Is there a fourth-order representation?

**No exact one at O(n³).** Propagating κ₄ needs one n×n block per neuron (ARC's B_r), each moved by a sandwich: n⁴ per layer. Lane M3 showed that this "repeated-slice transport" carries K=4's gain, and that low-rank B_r and single birth classes fail as width grows [M].

**But the ReLU step reads only three slices of κ₄:** D4 (n numbers), Q31 = κ₄(a,a,a,b) (n²) and D22 (n²).
- For blocks riding the κ₃ legs, D4 and D31 are O(n³) with no cross terms; D22 needs n⁴ (deep review §6.1) [M].
- κ₄'s value in our chain sits in Q31: exact Q31 gave −15.5 % unrefit in Anatomy 2 on v59 [M].
- In a separate twin experiment, all κ₄ slices together gave 7.6 % unrefit and 27.9 % after refit, i.e. refit multiplied the gain 3.7× [M].

So our fourth-order representation should be **readout-shaped**: carry κ₄ only as O(n²)-readout states and as birth blocks on legs we already have, and read only D4 and D31 from them. The pieces, cheapest first:

**1. Radial Sym(μ_pre⊗κ₃) term — new, free, O(n²).**
- A random overall gain with variance v adds, to first order, v·[6κ₄ + 12·Sym(C⊗C) + 12·Sym(μ_pre⊗κ₃)] to κ₄, where Sym is the average over index permutations. This is checked to O(v²) by `docs/research/2026-10-10_k4x/ward_check.py`.
- Our λ·C surrogate is the Sym(C⊗C) part. Nothing in v81d carries Sym(μ_pre⊗κ₃):
  - the Q31 surrogate (est:598–603) has no D21 or D3 term;
  - the closure's 15 D21-containing monomials are all second-order products;
  - only the fitted q3·α_a·α_b + q4 can absorb part of it [M, code read].
- Its readouts, with μ_pre = Wμ, D21_ab = κ₃(a,a,b) and D3 = κ₃(a,a,a):
  - Q31_ab += 3v·(3·μ_pre,a·D21_ab + μ_pre,b·D3_a)
  - D22_ab += 6v·(μ_pre,a·D21_ba + μ_pre,b·D21_ab)
  - D4_a += 12v·μ_pre,a·D3_a
- In the covariance update, the Q31 part becomes:
  - a row factor (1 − 3v·α_a²) on the first-order D21 term, about 1.5–6 % for kink neurons at v ≈ 0.003–0.005;
  - plus a rank-1 term (v/2)·w3_a·D3_a·w1_b·μ_pre,b.
- Supporting evidence: Puffi's best κ₄ modes contain the same D21- and D3-coupled forms [M].
- What does not support it: our own Anatomy 2 found the chain's λ term within 5–40 % of the label fit (below it at L16), so there is no direct sign of a missing term in our chain. The regression decides [M].

**2. Masked star-Q31 fold — zero extra products.**
- The star κ₄ born with each lineage rides the same legs and weights as star-D4. Its D31 readout is
  Q★_ab = Σ_j c4_j·(A_aj³·M_bj + 3·M_aj·A_aj²·A_bj)
  where c4_j is the per-lineage star weight (w3 at birth). Its right operands are the same as the D21 readout's.
- Adding (r_a/3)·(A∘A∘A)·diag(c4) to LP and r_a·(M∘A∘A)·diag(c4) to LA, with r = w3/w2 of the reading neuron, reproduces the covariance term ½·w2_a·w1_b·[D21 + (r_a/3)·Q★]_ab exactly, for the recent tier and the old tier [M, verified to 1e-15].
- D21 has five other consumers: the dressed leg H, the corrector feature d21e, births, the κ₄ core and closure.
- Masking the fold to rows with |α_a| ≤ 2 keeps 96–97 % of the intended term and cuts H and d21e contamination to 2–5 %. **Births still receive 0.3–0.5× of the term, about 80 % of it mismatched** (true Q31 on FIT net 0). It also steers the old-tier join, so it must be added after the join's weights are taken.

**3. Paid O(n³) pieces, youngest lineages only, and only if the regression says so:**
- path-D31: +1 product per lineage-layer;
- the (2,2)-block D31 on the M leg with the chain's K22: 2 u per block-layer;
- a Sym₆(M⊗C) state (kaileh57): 2 u per layer.

Each n-sized product costs about 100 ops, so these are limited by the 26k op gate as much as by F. 1 u ≈ 0.55 % F.

**How the pieces are chosen.**
- Run an offline regression of the true Q31 residual, and of the true D4 residual, on all candidates.
- Weight each pair by |w3_a·w1_b|·σ_a³·σ_b, i.e. by how Q31 enters the covariance. Report out-of-sample R² per layer.
- Rule of thumb, unrefit: Δraw ≈ −15.5 % × ΔR² / (1 − R²_now), where R²_now ≈ 0.5 for λ·C.

### 1.3 A better third-order representation?

**No exact format cheaper than our lineages exists.**
- ARC's factorization costs L²·n^K over the depth, and that is the floor. EscAI classified the alternatives and found none within 5 % on old-source D21 at ≤ 5 u per layer [M].
- Coherent forms failed:
  - Sym(v⊗C) retirement was 4.83× worse (EscAI);
  - our radial and harmonic tiers were 5.9× worse;
  - kaileh57's C-power fit works only at a small participation ratio [M].
- Compressing frozen birth factors needs ε ≤ 1.6 %, from EscAI's ε² law (extra MSE ≈ 4.2e-6·ε²). That is far beyond what k99 ranks deliver [M].
- Our rank-256 old tier costs only ×1.09 in ARC's chain [M].
- Why the coherent forms fail [I]: in an old lineage's readout, the coherent mean-direction part and the incoherent part are the same size. One is a coherent sum of 1/√n-sized components; the other is a √k random-sign sum. Dropping either one fails.

**What can be better is how we read κ₃, not how we store it.**

*Two-sided pruning: −8.84 % F alone, −8.0 % on top of the bundle.* Dead columns of D21, only non-dead output rows of every leg, and D3 = D4 = 0 on dead rows.
- **This reverses a recorded decision.** GOTCHAS #2 says to ship row-only D.
- P1's two-sided rows-and-columns mask (v73, per-net thresholds) cost +10.55 % with the corrector reading the masked d21e, and only +0.56 % with the corrector reading the unmasked feature (Dc2.5) [M].
- Fixed keep counts add about 1.1 points (P1 cont.) [M].
- Leg-row pruning and dead-row D3 = 0 were never measured.
- The only refit of a two-sided mask (P2 safe-d) was cancelled at +7.8 %, on a recipe whose own control spiked +6.8 % [M].
- The case for reopening it [I]:
  - the loss is the d21e feature, not physics;
  - scaling the kept-column d21e by a per-layer energy ratio calibrated on FIT may repair most of it with no refit, and that is a minutes-long twin test;
  - refits now work (v81d: −1.36 % from 2 epochs).
- P(lands) ≈ 0.45.

*Take the κ₄→κ₃ hub out of the legs: −6.03 % F.*
- Route (i), Sym3(w2 ⊗ (mλ/4)·D1·X̃·D1) with m the metric (= 2), is the only reason always-on birth columns are kept. In P1:
  - dropping dead and always-on columns cost +3.41 % (H2.5 literal);
  - dropping dead columns only cost +0.15 % (Hd2.5);
  - keeping only the hubs on always-on columns cost 0.00 % (Hkh), but that was measured at τ 3.0 only [M].
- Carrying route (i) as one O(n²) object, Sym3(U⊗C̃_pre) with per-age gains, would free those columns.
- Two checks come first:
  - **Hkh2.5** in the existing mask arm (minutes): the accuracy ceiling of the −6.03 % lever;
  - **a fidelity regression**, because the transported hub matrix differs from C̃ by inherited diagonal renewals of the same order.
- If age 1 must stay explicit (+8 % F), the move does not pay.

These two levers are the leaders' advantage we can see in the data [I]. Their FLOPs move 1–3 % from network to network, driven by a common network property. Our representation moves and reads rows and columns that dead and always-on neurons make worthless.

### 2.3 Physics facts from the code read [M]

**D21 has six consumers:**
1. the first-order covariance term (½·g14);
2. 15 of the 27 closure monomials, all second order, which feed covariance, births and the κ₄ core;
3. births (pK21 rows 18/19, the K21 conversion, the p111 subtraction);
4. the κ₄ core (pK22 row 23);
5. the dressed leg H = ½(w2_a·D21_aj + w1_a·r_j·D21_ja);
6. the corrector feature d21e.

**κ₄ in v81d:** κ₄ = m·Sym(G⊗I) with m = 2, giving D4 = m·dG + star, D22 = (m/6)(dG_a + dG_b) and Q31 = (m/2)λC. The closure's Q̂31 adds polynomials in ρ and α. Nothing couples κ₄ to κ₃.

**Route-(i) hub:** y = w2, c = (m/8)·λ·w1. 42 / 55 / 70 % of its column energy sits on always-on columns at L9 / L12 / L15.

**Lane M3, M10.1:** the K=4 gain is in exact transport of ARC's per-neuron blocks B_r. There is no O(n³) carrier for it.

## 7. Risks
