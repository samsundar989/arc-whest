# Skeptic verdict: ACCEPT, limited to the Gaussian-pair fourth source

The preregistered truth-free claim passes. A correlated unit-Gaussian pair produces nonzero mixed fourth cumulants after coordinatewise ReLU, and those terms change a fixed signed future-row D4 by more than the registered `1e-3` threshold. This accepts the n=2 Gaussian source and its exact tiny-tensor linear transport at the stated quadrature precision. It does not accept a later non-Gaussian source, a compressed residual, a challenge-cost model, accuracy, or deployment.

**Independent countercalculation.** I did not use the script's two-dimensional Gauss-Legendre density grid to compute the decisive values. For X=x>0, I integrated the conditional law `Y|X=x ~ N(rho*x, 1-rho^2)` in one dimension using adaptive quadrature. With `s=sqrt(1-rho^2)`, `t=rho*x/s`, its positive first and second moments are `rho*x*Phi(t)+s*phi(t)` and `((rho*x)^2+s^2)*Phi(t)+rho*x*s*phi(t)`. Integrating these against `x^a phi(x)` yields the mixed raw moments. At `rho=0`, `(m11,m21,m31,m22)=(0.15915494309189537, 0.19947114020071638, 0.3183098861837907, 0.25000000000000006)`; at `rho=0.5`, they are `(0.30449889052211476, 0.44881006545161195, 0.8101225036747582, 0.7067483357831721)`. These agree with the recorded order-96 moments to rounding. Marginal `mu=1/sqrt(2*pi)`, `m20=1/2`, `m30=2*mu`, and `m40=3/2` then give `K31=-5.55e-17`, `K22=0` at zero correlation, versus `K31=0.11417442414038248`, `K22=0.10914008267226882` at `rho=0.5`. The marginal `K4=0.27970833835270265` is unchanged. These values follow directly from the raw-moment fourth-cumulant partition polynomial; the script's separate central formula agrees.

For `B=[[1,1],[1,-1]]/sqrt(2)`, independent fourth-power expansion gives future D4 `[(2*K4+8*K31+6*K22)/4, (2*K4-8*K31+6*K22)/4]`. It is `[0.1398541691763512, 0.13985416917635143]` at `rho=0` and `[0.5319131414655195, 0.0752154449039896]` at `rho=0.5`. Keeping only source diagonals gives `K4/2=0.13985416917635132` in either future row. The correlated signed gaps are therefore `+0.3920589722891682` and `-0.06463872427236173`, agreeing with `result.json` to rounding and clearing the registered bound. The 64/96-grid result reports 80 passing checks, maximum material relative error `3.6623e-15`, and maximum absolute error `1.6653e-15`, below `1e-8`; my different one-dimensional integration also tests their common-bias risk at the decisive moments.

**Strongest counterexample to a broader claim.** The contraction-matched metric core is a projection, not the full source. With `M=2I`, symmetric tensor entries `K4=d`, `K31=a`, `K22=b` imply `G_11=G_22=3(d+b)/8` and `G_12=a`; its contraction matches by construction. At `rho=0`, the full future D4 is `0.139854169176351`, while the core alone is `0.209781253764527`, an error of `0.069927084588176` despite zero mixed source. At `rho=0.5`, the full minus core is about `+0.011927977416026` in each signed future row. The test retains `E=K-core`, so core plus residual and the contracted identity pass, but those checks cannot establish that E can be discarded or cheaply transported. A two-coordinate Gaussian pair also cannot establish the later-layer or multi-coordinate nonlinear closure.
